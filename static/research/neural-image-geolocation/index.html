Neural Image Geolocation with IA³

View the notebook · Portfolio

Cem Şirin · 2024 · TensorFlow / Keras

An experiment in adapting a pretrained vision transformer to image geolocation by aligning image and coordinate representations. I implemented IA³ adaptation for the vision encoder and a TensorFlow version of the GeoCLIP coordinate encoder, using transferred coordinate-encoder weights.

The contribution is the adaptation and training implementation. GeoCLIP supplies the underlying image-location alignment approach; the vision backbone is pretrained.

Task and data

Predict latitude and longitude from a street-level image. The original experiment uses the Streetview Image Dataset, approximately 25,000 geotagged images. Images are resized to 384 × 384 pixels for the vision transformer.

The saved code creates an 80/10/10 train/validation/test split with a fixed random seed. Evaluation reports the percentage of predictions within 1, 25, 200, 750, and 2,500 km of the reference location.

Model and training

  • Image encoder: a pretrained ViT-B/16 from vit-keras, extended with a custom IA³ implementation and an alignment head.
  • Coordinate encoder: a TensorFlow implementation of the GeoCLIP location encoder initialized from transferred weights.
  • Stage 1: train the image alignment head using mean squared error between image and location embeddings.
  • Stage 2: train the IA³ parameters and coordinate encoder using a contrastive objective based on symmetric cross entropy. The saved parameter selection also includes the alignment head and temperature.
  • Prediction: compare image embeddings against a gallery of candidate coordinate embeddings.

Both the trainable components and the objective change between stages. This experiment therefore measures the combined training procedure, rather than isolating the effect of IA³ alone.

Stage 1: alignment-head training

The saved plots show decreasing embedding MSE. Geographic accuracy remains limited, illustrating that lower alignment error alone does not guarantee useful geolocation.

Stage 2: joint adaptation

The saved plots show improved geographic accuracy after introducing joint adaptation and the contrastive objective. These are the original stored figure outputs; no training or plot data was regenerated during this presentation cleanup.

Interpretation and reproducibility

  • Joint adaptation improved the reported geographic accuracy over alignment-head training. The experiment does not establish an isolated IA³ effect or superiority over other fine-tuning methods.
  • The original notes report a plateau after roughly three to four epochs and no learning-rate scheduler. They do not establish why the plateau occurred.
  • This is a historical experiment with saved outputs, not a freshly reproduced benchmark. The original notebook does not record uncertainty across multiple seeds.
  • Re-running requires the companion image_encoder.py and location_encoder.py, dataset, transferred weights, and training-history files. Those dependencies are referenced by the original code and are not bundled in this presentation copy.
